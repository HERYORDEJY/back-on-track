# Capstone Theme: Learning That Stays With You

Access to knowledge, skills, and opportunity increasingly depends on whether people can find the right learning resources, understand how to use them, and stay engaged long enough to benefit. Yet for many students across Africa, learning materials are scattered, locked behind paywalls or logins, hard to discover, poorly matched to individual needs, or simply overwhelming to navigate. Too many people start a learning journey and quietly fall off it.

### Choose from three challenge tracks:

1. Access & Discovery of Learning Resources: Technology that automates how learners find, retrieve, and organize the materials they need, making quality resources easier to reach and use.
2. Engagement & Staying in the Learning Journey: Tools that help learners stay motivated and keep going, so they remain in the learning process over time rather than falling off.
3. Guidance, Pathways & Opportunity: Tools that help learners understand where their learning can take them and what to do next, connecting study to real progress. Cross-track entries are welcome.

Project Scope Using AI software development tools, build a solution that responds to a real learning-access challenge within one or more of the tracks above. This is intentionally broad to give you room to explore the needs of different learners and communities and develop an original approach.

Your project should do more than deliver content. It should help people engage with learning in a way that is sustained, useful, and connected to opportunity.

Tip: This is an invention sprint, not a product launch. You are not expected to build production-ready software. You are expected to use AI software development tools to create a working proof of concept that demonstrates your idea is useful, viable, and worth developing further. Please refrain from using AI to generate your capstone idea.

Operating Constraints Your solution should account for the real conditions experienced by the learners it is designed to serve:

Quality and credibility: Learning materials must be accurate and traceable to credible sources. Low bandwidth and limited access: Solutions should remain useful in areas with unreliable internet, limited mobile data, or basic devices. Accessibility and inclusion: Consider learners with different literacy levels, disabilities, levels of digital confidence, and access to technology. Personalization without exclusion: Tools that adapt to a learner's level or pace are powerful, but they should not lock out those who do not fit a standard profile. Privacy and security: Learners may share personal, academic, or performance information. Multilingual access: Learners work across diverse languages, including English, French, Portuguese, Arabic, and numerous local languages. Local relevance: Curricula, credentials, institutions, and opportunities vary by location.

## Idea Submittied by Me
Many students at universities like MIVA fit their studies around full-time jobs, businesses and children. One hard month and they're 3 weeks behind. Catching up starts to look impossible, and that's when students quietly drop out.

Back on Track builds a catch-up plan around the hours a student actually has. A nurse on night shifts with 40 minutes on weeknights gets a plan that fits 40 minutes. It decides which topics to keep, shorten or skip, and each decision shows the course fact behind it, such as "needed for Assignment 2, due 1 November". Her goal, a promotion to senior nurse, sits at the top of the plan.

When she misses a session, the plan adjusts. If the numbers say she can't catch up before the exam, it tells her plainly, suggests a study break and alerts her advisor. The AI makes the judgement calls on what to cut; the code does the arithmetic and checks every plan.

MIVA has enrolled more than 20,000 students since 2023 and aims for 100,000 by 2027. No advisor team can personally follow that many people, so Back on Track gives advisors a list of who needs a call this week and why, with a message already drafted in the student's language.

Once loaded, it keeps working offline on basic phones, in English and Nigerian Pidgin. It can also read the plan aloud in English, for students with poor eyesight or anyone listening on the go. Student names never go to the AI.
